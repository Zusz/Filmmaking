## Exposition

### (Read this only if something doesn't make sense. Otherwise I'd skip it for the mystery, or read it at the end.)

> Picks up basically right where Ep2 ended — same afternoon/evening. Rell's ship and June's ship are arriving at the outside trade hall (the "outpost") from the Ep2 cliffhanger.

> **Trade outposts** (working name, the "trade hall" from Ep2) are Spire-controlled posts the Spire has been building *outside* the Outer Spire as it expands. They aren't just markets — every crystal that moves through one gets logged and taxed. It's how the Spire keeps track of where its stones end up. So a post like this is half-market, half-customs house. Smaller ships get inspected at the dock before they're let in to trade.

> **Cut-off clusters** (new this episode): because the Mothercrystal is dimming and the Spire is rationing, the Spire has quietly stopped supplying its farthest/least useful clusters — letting them "go dark" so the closer ones (the Outer Spire) stay lit. Officially this isn't happening. Unofficially, the only crystals those abandoned clusters can still get come from people like Rell. This is the thing that complicates Wren's whole plan: she's flying to *beg* the Spire for a crystal, and she's about to learn what the Spire does to clusters that beg.

> **The patrol bulletin:** the patrol vessel they escaped in Ep2 (Scene 16) reported the little ship that fled. That description travels post-to-post. So the outpost is loosely on the lookout for a small ship matching June's — hand-carved crest, fresh hull damage. This is the complication in the middle of the episode.

> **"The fastest way in"** (set up by Hask in Ep2 Scene 13 — "we don't fly where everyone else flies"): the unofficial route into the *Inner* Spire, past the wall/guards/cannons. Rell's real reason for stopping at this outpost is a contact who can give her that route. The crate job is the *price* for it. **Why** Rell wants into the Inner Spire so badly is still the mystery (tied to whatever Hask warned her about in Ep2 Scene 9 — "what happened last time"). Not answered this episode.

### Previous exposition still relevant:

> The Sundering scattered the world into floating islands held up by crystals. Clusters are little groups of islands held together by one crystal (Tethermoor, the Spire). The Spire = the central cluster / its main island / the government, which holds power by controlling crystals.

> Crystals are grown on the Mothercrystal and handed out by the Spire. They make islands and ships *float*; ships still need an engine to *move*. Older ships float on balloons instead (like June's).

> The Mothercrystal is dimming — output down ~a quarter percent a night (Ep1 Scene 1, Ep2 Scene 1). The Spire is handing out far more crystals than it can grow, and the reserves are nearly gone. Brask wants ration cuts; Vey keeps stalling.

> Tethermoor's housing crystal is cracked **from the inside** ("starved," per Yara). That's the clock on the whole show.

### Thread C-adjacent — the outpost

- **Calder** — Rell's contact at the outpost. **Male** (flippable). A fixer who lives behind a customs counter and survives by never being interesting. The *opposite* of Bram — where Bram performs a big life he didn't have, Calder shrinks himself on purpose. Nervous, fast under pressure, glances over his shoulder mid-sentence. Owes Rell, or is owed by her; it's never quite clear which. Voice should be quiet, clipped, a little jumpy, the speech of someone always half-listening for footsteps. Warm enough underneath that you believe he actually cares where the crystals end up.

- **INSPECTOR** — functional role, like the Patrol Officer in Ep2 (keep casting light, can reuse that actor/skin). **Either.** Spire dock inspector at the outpost. By-the-book, a little bored, dangerous mostly because the rules are on their side. Voice should be flat, official, the tone of someone reading off a list they didn't write.

## Scene 1 — The Spire, Mothercrystal Vault
*(Thread B. ~70 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** The vault again (reuse Ep1 Scene 1). The Mothercrystal hangs on its ropes, glowing — but maybe a touch dimmer than Ep1, if we can manage it visually. Vey stands at the lectern, a stack of papers in front of her. Brask enters. He doesn't have to say the number; she's already read it.

> **BRASK:** You have the reading now.

> **VEY:** *(not looking up)* I have the reading.

> **BRASK:** And?

> **VEY:** *(quiet)* You were right. I didn't like it.

*(Brask doesn't gloat. He just steps closer, lowers his voice — this is the version of him that's actually frightened, which is worse than the loud one.)*

> **BRASK:** Then we cut. Tonight.

> **VEY:** We cut. *(a beat)* But not evenly.

> **BRASK:** *(carefully)* ...Go on.

> **VEY:** *(turning a page, reading flatly, like it costs her nothing — which means it costs her everything)* We hold the Outer Spire full. We trim the middle. And the far clusters — the ones at the end of every route, the ones we already barely reach — *(small pause)* we stop.

> **BRASK:** Stop sending entirely.

> **VEY:** Until the Mothercrystal recovers.

> **BRASK:** *(the smallest hesitation, even for him)* And if it doesn't recover?

*(Vey finally looks up at him. She doesn't answer that. She slides the top sheet across the lectern toward him instead.)*

> **VEY:** The petitions hall. How many came in this week.

> **BRASK:** *(glancing, reading)* ...That's more than last month entire.

> **VEY:** Every one of them is a cluster whose crystal started cracking. *(quiet)* They all think they're the only one. They all think if they just *ask*, we'll send someone.

> **BRASK:** *(flat)* We can't send someone to all of them.

> **VEY:** No. *(looks back at the Mothercrystal)* We can barely send the clerks out to tell them *no.*

*(A pause. Brask sets the sheet down.)*

> **BRASK:** I'll draft the cut order.

> **VEY:** Quietly, Brask. *(a beat)* If the far clusters find out we've already written them off, they stop petitioning and start coming up the wall.

*(Brask exits. Vey stands alone in front of the dimming crystal. Hold a second. Cut.)*

## Scene 2 — The outpost, dock
*(Thread A & C. ~80 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Picking up from Ep2 Scene 18. A big trade hall built on a larger cluster, with a giant dock. Both ships are coming in — Rell's larger scrappy vessel, June's little balloon ship roped behind it. The dock is busier than anything we've seen: stalls, crates, a couple of other small ships, a posted board near the dock entrance with notices pinned to it. Rell hops the gap onto June's deck as they ease in, completely at home.

> **RELL:** *(grinning, gesturing at the whole place)* There she is. Civilisation. Or the Spire's idea of it, anyway.

> **WREN:** *(eyeing the board, the stalls, all of it)* You said one job. *(flat)* This is the job?

> **RELL:** This is *where* the job is. The job itself is the easiest gold ya'll ever not-quite-earn.

> **JUNE:** *(tying off, wary)* You keep saying easy. Every time someone says easy, I end up holding something on fire.

> **RELL:** *(laughs)* Then ye've been keepin' bad company. *(she crouches, pats a sealed crate sitting on June's deck — Hask must have loaded it during the crossing)* This. It goes off your ship, across that dock, to a fella named Calder behind the customs counter. That's it. That's the whole favour.

*(Wren looks at the crate. It's plain, roped shut, unmarked. She doesn't like that it's already on her ship.)*

> **WREN:** When did that get on my ship?

> **JUNE:** *(under her breath)* It's *my* ship.

> **WREN:** *(not breaking eye contact with Rell)* When did that get on June's ship.

> **RELL:** *(easy)* On the crossing. Figured I'd save us the carrying. *(beat, reading Wren's face)* It's *cargo*, love. Folk move cargo. It's the most ordinary thing in the sky.

> **WREN:** Then why's it on the ship nobody official looks at twice, instead of yours?

*(A flicker — Rell's caught, but she doesn't flinch. Hask, stepping over from his ship, answers before Rell has to. Flat.)*

> **HASK:** Because they look at *ours* twice.

> **RELL:** *(spreading her hands, like it's nothing)* We're known here. Bein' known's good for trade and terrible for crossing a checkpoint. You two are gloriously, beautifully *un*known. *(claps Wren on the shoulder)* Smile, hand Calder the crate, and by tonight I point you the fast way to the Spire. One favour. Then we're square.

*(Wren holds the look a second longer. She doesn't say yes. She just picks up one end of the crate. June gets the other.)*

> **WREN:** *(quiet, to June)* One job.

> **JUNE:** *(quiet back)* Then we're done. Yeah. You keep saying that too.

## Scene 3 — Tethermoor, the crystal house
*(Thread A — home. ~40 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Back home. The crystal house from Ep1 Scene 2. Evening light. Pip is alone with the cluster crystal, sitting cross-legged in front of it with one of Wren's tools held in both hands — not using it, just holding it, the way you hold something that belongs to someone who's away. The crack down the center is visibly worse than Ep1. A faint cracking sound.

*(Pip leans in close, listening, then talks to the crystal like it can hear — bright, nervous, talking-too-much-because-scared.)*

> **PIP:** Okay. So. Wren said keep an eye on you. That's the job. So I'm — I'm keeping an eye. *(beat)* You're supposed to just glow and float and be *fine.* That's *your* job.

> **PIP:** ...That one was bigger than yesterday.

*(Pip stands, backs toward the door, not taking their eyes off the crystal — then turns and runs out. Hold on the empty room and the crack a moment. Nice cut.)*

## Scene 4 — The outpost, dock (the board)
*(Thread A & C. ~65 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Crossing the dock toward the customs counter, Wren and June carrying the crate, Rell ahead of them, Hask trailing. They pass the notice board near the dock entrance. Wren — being Wren — actually reads it. She slows. Stops.

> **JUNE:** *(strained, holding her end)* Why are we stopping. The crate doesn't get lighter while you read.

*(Wren's looking at one notice in particular. A description — no picture, just words. We don't have to show the text; we show her face change.)*

> **WREN:** *(quiet)* June. Look at this one.

> **JUNE:** *(leans, reads, goes still)* ..."Small ship. Balloon lift. Hand-carved crest, starboard." *(beat)* "Fresh hull damage."

*(They both look back, instinctively, at June's little ship roped at the dock. Hand-carved crest. The patched hole in the hull from the rough landing in Ep2. It matches. It matches exactly.)*

> **JUNE:** *(low)* That's us. That's *describing us.*

> **WREN:** *(turning on Rell, controlled but sharp)* That patrol we ran from. They reported the ship.

> **RELL:** *(glancing at the board, unbothered — too unbothered)* ...Aye. They do that.

> **WREN:** You knew.

> **RELL:** I *suspected.* *(beat, lighter)* It's a busy board, love. Half the ships in the sky have a crest and a dent. They're not gonna —

> **WREN:** *(cutting her off, fast and quiet)* They are if they read it off a list. *(she's already setting her end of the crate down, already looking at the ship, already solving it)* We can't take it through inspection looking like that.

> **HASK:** *(flat, a little impressed despite himself)* She's right. The dent's the tell. The crest's the tell.

> **RELL:** *(to Wren, testing)* And what d'ya want to do about it? We can't exactly *un-fly* through that checkpoint. Calder's on the other side of it.

*(Wren's already moving back toward the ship. Over her shoulder — and this is the difference between her and Rell, she fixes the thing instead of fooling the man:)*

> **WREN:** I'm not going to lie to the inspector. *(beat)* I'm going to make the ship stop matching the list.

> **JUNE:** *(catching up, getting it, half a grin)* You're gonna re-skin her.

> **WREN:** *(picking the crate back up)* New crest panel. Finish the hull patch so it reads old, not fresh. *(to June)* You carved the crest in. You can carve a different one.

> **JUNE:** *(mock-wounded, following)* That crest was *art.*

> **WREN:** It was a duck, June.

> **JUNE:** *(offended)* It was a *heron.*

*(They head back to the ship. Rell watches them go, something thoughtful — not worried — on her face.)*

> **RELL:** *(quietly, to Hask)* ...She doesn't even cheat the *easy* way.

> **HASK:** *(flat)* No. *(beat)* I noticed.

## Scene 5 — Tethermoor, the bar
*(Thread D. ~50 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** The bar (reuse). Bram is NOT in it — he's outside the door, which we see through the doorway, pacing the little dockfront with a knapsack, mid-grand-departure-that-isn't-happening. Tova watches from behind the bar, drying a mug, with the patience of someone who has seen this exact thing before.

*(Bram strides up to the doorway, full of purpose, then stops, deflates, comes halfway in.)*

> **BRAM:** *(declaring)* Tova. I require a ship.

> **TOVA:** *(not looking up)* They're at the dock. Big floaty things. Can't miss them.

> **BRAM:** I require a ship that is *mine.* For my adventure. *(grandly)* I've decided to earn one. With honest labour and the sweat of —

> **TOVA:** With what gold?

*(Bram opens his mouth. Closes it. Pats his pockets, theatrically, knowing full well they're empty.)*

> **BRAM:** *(recovering)* Gold is a *small* part of acquiring a ship. There's also reputation. Standing. A captain's *bearing.*

> **TOVA:** You have a knapsack with one spare boot in it. I watched you pack.

> **BRAM:** *(stung)* A spare boot is *preparedness.*

> **TOVA:** *(flat)* For when you lose the other one being dramatic on a dock.

*(Bram draws himself up. Tries one more time, with feeling.)*

> **BRAM:** I have crewed twenty ships —

> **TOVA:** You sold a twenty-ship fleet down to one ship and then down to a barrel of bread.

> **BRAM:** *(a long pause, the wind genuinely out of him)* ...The bread was for a *good cause.* I was thirsty.

*(He sits down heavily, just inside the door, adventure stalled at the threshold. Tova slides him a mug of water — not a drink. He looks at it, betrayed. Cut.)*

## Scene 6 — The outpost, the inspection
*(Thread A & C. ~95 seconds. Max on-screen: 5 — Wren, June, Rell, Hask, Inspector.)*

**SETTING:** The customs checkpoint at the head of the dock — a counter/gate the inspector works from, ships brought up one at a time. June's ship has been quietly worked over: a NEW crest panel (different shape from the duck/heron), the hull patch sanded/weathered so it reads old. Wren, June, Rell, and Hask stand with the ship as the INSPECTOR approaches, a list in hand. Hask carries the crate now, holding it like it weighs nothing and means nothing.

> **INSPECTOR:** *(bored, official)* Inspection before entry. Ship, origin, cargo.

> **RELL:** *(stepping up first, easy, charming)* Evening! Two ships, travelling together. Goods for trade. The big handsome one's ours —

> **INSPECTOR:** *(not looking at Rell, looking at June's little ship)* The small one.

*(A flicker between Wren and June. Here it is.)*

> **JUNE:** *(stepping up, casual)* Mine. Balloon rig. Came up from the outer routes.

*(The Inspector consults the list. We can feel them checking it against the ship. Crest — doesn't match the listed one. Hull — reads old, not fresh. They frown, like the shape of a thought won't quite land.)*

> **INSPECTOR:** *(half to themselves)* ...We had a notice. Small ship, balloon, crest, hull damage.

> **WREN:** *(even, true, not a lie)* Lot of small ships out here with crests and old dents.

> **INSPECTOR:** *(looking at the hull)* This damage is old.

> **WREN:** *(flat)* Older than the notice, probably.

*(The Inspector's eyes move along the hull — and stop. Right at the edge of the patch, where the sanding's a little too clean, a little too recent. They lean in. Beat of real danger.)*

> **INSPECTOR:** *(slowly)* This patch, though. This is —

*(Hask, without being asked, sets the crate down on the counter with a solid, attention-pulling thunk — right in the Inspector's eyeline. Flat, helpful, the most words he's said in a row all episode:)*

> **HASK:** Cargo's logged through Calder. Crystals-and-sundries manifest, counter's expecting it. *(beat)* You want to inspect the patch or you want to log the crate before he closes the window? *(beat)* Your post, your call. Just know he closes the window.

*(The magic word is "Calder." And "the window." The Inspector straightens, recalculating — a name they know, a deadline they don't want to be on the wrong side of. The patch becomes less interesting than the paperwork. Bureaucracy beats suspicion.)*

> **INSPECTOR:** *(grumbling, stamping the list)* ...Log it through the counter. Both ships, in. Don't loiter on the dock.

> **RELL:** *(beaming)* Wouldn't dream of it.

*(The Inspector waves the next ship up and moves off. The four of them exhale — Wren slowest of all. June grabs the crate before Hask can.)*

> **JUNE:** *(quiet, to Wren, half-laughing with leftover adrenaline)* The patch. They nearly had us on the *patch.*

> **WREN:** *(watching the Inspector go, quiet)* I sanded it too clean. *(beat, almost to herself)* I'll remember that.

> **RELL:** *(low, genuinely a little awed, as they walk in)* Ya didn't tell them a single lie back there.

> **WREN:** *(flat, not a brag, just true)* Didn't have to. The ship told the truth. I just changed what the truth was.

*(Rell laughs under her breath — but there's a beat where she looks at Wren like she's filing that away. Cut.)*

## Scene 7 — The outpost, behind the counter (Calder)
*(Thread A & C. ~80 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** A cramped back room behind the customs counter — Calder's space. Shelves, ledgers, a curtain instead of a door. Calder is exactly the man you'd build to never be noticed: small movements, eyes that keep flicking to the curtain. The crate's on the table between them. Rell's relaxed. Wren and June hover at the edge. (Hask stays out front by the ships — keeps us at 4 in here, and gives Hask a reason to be watching the dock for Scene 9 later.)

> **CALDER:** *(low, fast, to Rell, not really greeting her)* You're late, and you brought *strangers.*

> **RELL:** *(warm)* I brought a *clean ship*, Calder, which is more than you asked for and exactly what you needed. *(gestures at the crate)* Same as always. Counted, sealed, yours.

*(Calder unties the crate. Lifts the lid. Inside — and now we see it — crystals. Smaller stones, the airship/housing kind, glowing faintly. A whole crate of them. Wren goes very still.)*

> **WREN:** *(quiet, the floor shifting under her)* ...Those are crystals.

> **CALDER:** *(barely glancing up, counting)* Eleven. Good. *(to Rell)* Two short of what I —

> **RELL:** *(smooth)* Two went to a cluster on the way that couldn't wait for *you* to count them.

*(Calder stops. Looks at her. Doesn't argue. He closes the crate, almost gentle with it.)*

> **WREN:** *(stepping in, low, urgent)* Where are these going.

> **CALDER:** *(wary, to Rell)* Why's she asking.

> **RELL:** *(quiet, watching Wren)* Because she's honest, and honest people always ask the question they're afraid of. *(to Wren, simply)* Tell her, Calder. She's earned the answer; she carried 'em past the gate.

*(Calder hesitates, glances at the curtain, then — quieter, and for the first time something real and tired underneath the jumpiness:)*

> **CALDER:** They go *out.* Past the routes. To the clusters the Spire stopped sending to.

> **WREN:** The Spire stopped — *(beat)* The Spire doesn't stop. They *distribute.* That's the whole —

> **CALDER:** *(flat, the truth of it)* The Spire distributes to who's worth keeping lit. *(beat)* The far clusters started going dark a season ago. No notice. The ration just... didn't come. *(nods at the crate)* This is the only ration they get now. And it's stolen, salvaged, and smuggled, every stone.

*(Silence. Wren looks at the crate of glowing crystals — the exact thing she's flying across the sky to beg the Spire for — and it's a crate of contraband on a smuggler's table. June reads her friend's face and says nothing.)*

> **RELL:** *(gently, to Calder, business)* My end's done. Now yours. The way in.

*(Calder reaches under the table, slides a small folded paper — or a marked token — across to Rell. He does not look happy to part with it.)*

> **CALDER:** *(barely above a whisper)* That gets you to the wall. What you do at the wall is your funeral, not mine. *(beat, a look at Rell that has history in it)* Same as last time.

*(Rell pockets it without a word. The "last time" lands — Wren clocks it, June clocks it — but Rell's already turning away. Cut.)*

## Scene 8 — Tethermoor, Yara's house
*(Thread A — home. ~45 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Yara's cluttered, warm house (reuse Ep1 Scene 3). Pip has run here. Doss is already inside — clearly the two adults left behind end up here a lot now. Yara's at the shelves; Doss is sitting heavily, the man who has to be calm for everyone else.

> **PIP:** *(rushing in, words tumbling)* It's worse. The crack. It's *louder* and it's *longer* and I counted and it's past the middle now and I didn't touch it I *promise* I just —

> **DOSS:** *(steady, gentle, the soft-around-Pip voice)* Breathe, Pip. You didn't do anything.

> **PIP:** *(stopping, smaller)* ...It's getting worse faster. Isn't it.

*(Doss looks at Yara. He wants her to say no. Yara doesn't do that — she takes her time, and when she speaks it's quiet and true, which is worse than loud.)*

> **YARA:** *(slow)* Yes. It is.

> **DOSS:** *(low, to Yara, not for Pip)* How long.

> **YARA:** *(simply)* Longer than it'd take Wren to reach the Spire. Shorter than I'd like. *(beat)* I won't pretend at a number, Doss. I've seen one crystal die. I was a girl. It doesn't announce itself, and then it announces itself all at once.

*(Pip looks between them. Then, doing the brave thing, the Wren thing:)*

> **PIP:** Then I keep watching it. So when Wren's keeper comes, they know everything. *(beat, quiet)* That's my job.

*(Yara crosses and puts a hand on Pip's head — that distant-but-warm thing she does.)*

> **YARA:** *(soft)* That's a good job. *(beat)* And it's hers to fix. Not yours. You only have to *watch.*

*(Doss exhales, looks out the window toward the horizon, toward the Spire, the same shot Wren took in Ep1 Scene 4 — but from home now. Hold. Cut.)*

## Scene 9 — The outpost, the dock (Wren confronts Rell)
*(Thread A & C. ~85 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Back out on the dock by the ships, dusk now. Hask is nearby, ostensibly checking lines but really keeping watch. June's loading the patched ship. Wren walks straight up to Rell — quiet, controlled, the leak-at-the-edges anger.

> **WREN:** The patrol. In Ep— *(catches herself; she means the crossing)* On the crossing. The coupling fault. The "lucky timing."

> **RELL:** *(easy, not pretending)* Aye.

> **WREN:** That was you.

> **RELL:** *(a small shrug)* That was me. *(beat)* You knew on the day. I saw it on your face. You just decided not to know.

*(That lands — because it's true. Wren's jaw tightens.)*

> **WREN:** You shot a hole in a Spire ship with us tied alongside.

> **RELL:** *(calm, stepping closer, and now the grey of her comes out — reasonable, which is the unsettling part)* I'll tell ya exactly what'd have happened if I hadn't. He opens the hold. He finds a crate of crystals with no Spire stamp. He impounds *both* ships — yours included. He holds the four of us a week while he sends word up the line. *(beat)* And your cluster's crystal cracks the rest of the way through while you sit in a cell at a checkpoint, being *honest.*

*(Wren has no answer to that, and hates it.)*

> **RELL:** *(quieter)* You can be angry about *how* I got you out of that. Or you can be home in time to matter. *(beat)* Pick one. Don't pretend ya can have both.

> **WREN:** *(low, the real thing underneath)* You smuggle crystals.

> **RELL:** Aye.

> **WREN:** I'm flying to the Spire to *ask* them for a crystal. Legally. The right way. And you've got a crate of them under a curtain going to clusters they left to die.

> **RELL:** *(and this is the line that breaks her plan open, said gently, not cruel)* ...Then ask yourself somethin', on the way. *(beat)* All those far clusters that went dark — every one of them sent a clean little petition to that same hall you're flying toward. Polite. Honest. The right way. *(beat)* Where d'ya think those folk get their crystals *now?*

*(Long beat. Wren looks at the patched ship, at the dark sky toward the Spire, the certainty she left home with visibly cracking — like the crystal, from the inside.)*

> **WREN:** *(barely)* Why do you want into the Inner Spire, Rell.

*(Rell's face closes — the friendly door shutting. Hask, in the background, stops coiling rope and looks over, just slightly. The warning from before, unspoken.)*

> **RELL:** *(light again, deflecting, walking off)* One favour at a time, love. *(over her shoulder)* You did yours. Mine's my own.

*(Wren watches her go. Hask, beside her now, flat, almost kind:)*

> **HASK:** Don't pull that thread. *(beat)* Last person who did is why she doesn't answer it.

*(Cut.)*

## Scene 10 — The Spire, petitions hall (corridor)
*(Thread B. ~40 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** A different corner of the Spire — outside the petitions hall. Stacks of filed petitions on a clerk's desk, more than the desk can hold. This is where we lightly plant **Sela** (the clerk Doss told Wren to ask for, Ep1 Scene 4) — we don't have to over-introduce her; a name on a beat is enough. Brask passes through with the cut order; a Clerk (Sela) is half-buried in paper.

> **BRASK:** *(to the clerk, not slowing)* The far-cluster petitions. Stop processing them.

> **SELA:** *(looking up, overwhelmed, a clerk who still cares, which is the tragedy)* Stop — Councilor, some of these came in *weeks* ago. People are waiting for an answer.

> **BRASK:** *(flat)* They have one. It's no. *(beat)* You just don't have to write it down anymore.

> **SELA:** *(quiet, gesturing at the stacks)* And when they keep coming? When one of them sends someone *here*, to the hall, in person —

> **BRASK:** *(already walking, over his shoulder)* Then you'll do what this hall does best, clerk. *(beat)* You'll keep them waiting.

*(Sela looks down at the mountain of petitions — each one a cluster, each one someone like Wren. She doesn't file the cut order. She just sets it on top of the pile and stares at it. Cut.)*

> *(Note: this is the same hall, and the same clerk, Wren's been told to ask for. So when she arrives next episode, we've already seen what's waiting for her: a no, written or not.)*

## Scene 11 — Tethermoor, the bar
*(Thread D. ~55 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** The bar (reuse). Bram is still at the threshold table from Scene 5, adventure thoroughly stalled, the mug of water untouched and accusatory in front of him. Tova's wiping down the counter. A long quiet — for these two, almost companionable.

> **BRAM:** *(staring at the water, defeated, then — a man reaching for the last shred)* ...You said you were looking for someone. To clean the bar.

> **TOVA:** *(not looking up)* I said that, yeah.

> **BRAM:** *(carefully, like it physically hurts)* What does it... pay.

> **TOVA:** Room in the back. Meals. *(beat)* And I don't tell anyone the heron was a duck.

> **BRAM:** *(a flicker of the old fire)* It was a *career*, that ship, I'll have you —

> **TOVA:** *(flat)* The job, Bram. Yes or no.

*(Bram stands. We expect deflation. Instead — because he's Bram — he reframes total failure as the heroic first chapter of a saga, gesturing grandly at a mop leaning in the corner.)*

> **BRAM:** *(rising into it, voice building)* Then hear me, Tova. Every great captain *started* somewhere humble. Scrubbing decks. Hauling rope. *(grabbing the mop like a boarding pike)* This — *this* is not a mop. This is the *first rung* of a ladder that ends with me on the deck of a ship I have *earned!*

> **TOVA:** *(deadpan)* It's a mop.

> **BRAM:** *(undeterred, marching toward a table to clean it, narrating his own legend)* "And so the great Bram, brought low by fate and a generous nature, took up the humble mop, not knowing it was the beginning of his —"

> **TOVA:** *(cutting him off, but there's the smallest warmth in it now)* You missed a spot. *(beat)* Right where you spilled the bread crumbs storming out earlier.

*(Bram pauses, looks at the spot, looks at her — and, for once, doesn't argue. Starts cleaning. Tova almost — almost — smiles, and goes back to the counter. Cut.)*

## Scene 12 — The outpost, departure
*(Thread A & C. ~75 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Night. The two ships untying from the outpost dock. June's small ship — patched, re-crested, flying truer than it has all series. Rell's ship alongside. The lit windows of the trade hall behind them; the open dark sky and, far ahead, the faintest glow on the horizon: the Spire. Ropes are being strung between the two ships again (callback to Ep2 Scene 14).

> **JUNE:** *(at the controls, lighter than Wren, but watching her friend)* You've been quiet since the counter. *(beat)* Quieter than crate-on-my-ship quiet.

> **WREN:** *(looking out at the horizon glow)* I left home sure of one thing, June. That if I just got to the Spire and *asked*, the right way, they'd help. *(beat)* That the Spire was the place you *go* when your crystal's dying.

> **JUNE:** *(carefully)* ...And now?

> **WREN:** *(quiet)* Now I've met a man with a crate of crystals he has to *steal* to keep clusters alive that already asked. The right way. And got nothing. *(beat)* So I don't know what I'm flying toward anymore. Help, or just a longer line.

*(June doesn't have a joke for that. She lets it sit. Then, because June's whole job in Wren's life is to be the one who keeps moving:)*

> **JUNE:** Then we find out. *(beat)* You still have to ask, Wren. Even if the answer might be no. Pip's watching that crystal because *you* told them it'd be okay. *(gently)* We go ask. And if they say no — *(nods at Rell's ship)* well. We've gone and made friends with the people who know what to do when the Spire says no.

*(Wren looks over at Rell's ship. Rell's at her own helm, the folded token/paper from Calder visible in her hand, looking ahead at the Spire glow with an expression that has nothing to do with trade. Whatever's behind that wall, it's personal for her.)*

*(Rell catches Wren watching. Calls across the gap between the ships — back to her easy self, but there's an edge of something honest in it.)*

> **RELL:** *(calling over)* Strap in, Tethermoor! *(holds up the token)* This is the fast way. *(beat, grinning)* It is *not* the safe way.

> **WREN:** *(calling back, dry, but steadier now — she's chosen to keep going)* You said fast *and* safe. On the dock. I remember.

> **RELL:** *(laughing into the wind)* Aye, I say a lot of things on a dock! *(beat, more real)* The way nobody flies, love. That's the only door left that opens. *(turning to her helm)* Last stretch. Keep close, and don't ask me what's at the wall till we can see it!

*(Both ships pull forward into the dark, roped together, toward the distant glow.)*

## Scene 13 — Open sky, toward the Spire
*(Thread A. ~40 seconds.)*

**SETTING:** Wide shot. The two little ships, roped together, small against an enormous night sky — and ahead, growing, the Spire. For the first time we see real scale: the *Inner* Spire and its **wall**, ringed by its smaller islands, and — if we can manage it — the faint shapes of cannons along the top, pointed at the sky. The thing that shoots down anyone coming over or under. Wren stands at the rail, the wind in her face, looking at it.

> **WREN:** *(quiet, mostly to herself — the Ep1 callback, but flipped)* Marl says the island held the Hallowsen quake fine. *(beat)* I used to think the Spire was the thing that *held.* The thing that kept us all up.

*(The wall gets closer. The cannons resolve out of the dark. June, beside her, sees them too and stops grinning.)*

> **JUNE:** *(low)* ...Wren. The top of the wall.

> **WREN:** *(seeing them, very still)* I see them.

*(Beat. The glow of the Spire on both their faces. Wren doesn't look away from the cannons.)*

> **WREN:** *(quiet, final)* Now I'm flying *into* the thing that holds everyone down. *(beat)* The way nobody flies.

*(Hold on the wide shot — the two tiny roped ships, and the enormous walled Spire bristling ahead of them, getting closer. Cut to black.)*

**[End of Episode 3]**

## Where this leaves the threads (for me, planning Ep4)

- **A (Wren):** Has the route to the Inner Spire (via Rell/Calder) but has *lost her certainty* about what the Spire even is. Arrives at the wall next episode. Open question she's carrying: will the Spire help, or is she just a politer version of the clusters that already got told no?
- **B (Spire):** Cut order is out; far clusters officially abandoned; petitions hall told to stall people in person. So the Spire has, off-screen, already decided the answer to Wren's petition before she arrives. Dramatic irony loaded for Ep4. Sela planted.
- **C (Traders):** Rell's real motive (the Inner Spire, "last time," the thing Hask won't let Wren ask about) is now openly the central mystery. Still withheld.
- **D (Bram/Tova):** Bram has taken the cleaning job — "step one" of earning a ship. This is the start of his actual arc and gives the bar a reason to keep being a location. Could split into its own home-front thread whenever I want.
- **Home (Pip/Yara/Doss):** The clock is explicit now — Yara's basically said the crystal won't last much longer than it takes Wren to get there. So every delay next episode has weight.
